## R&D in Ireland: short explanation

For Ireland's R&D corporation tax credit, work is not qualifying just because it is new to a company, technically difficult, or software development. Revenue's guidance asks whether the company carried out systematic, investigative or experimental activities in a field of science or technology, seeking a scientific or technological advancement and trying to resolve scientific or technological uncertainty. The work should fall within basic research, applied research or experimental development.

In practical terms, there should be a genuine technical question whose answer or route to an answer was not readily available to a competent professional. The team should have investigated it in a planned way, testing or evaluating possible approaches. Routine maintenance, ordinary bug fixing, configuration, documentation, and implementation of known solutions are not R&D merely because they require skill. An unsuccessful investigation can still be relevant; success alone does not establish eligibility.

See [Revenue's Tax and Duty Manual, Part 29-02-03](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-29/29-02-03.pdf) and [Revenue's R&D Tax Credit Guidelines](https://www.revenue.ie/en/companies-and-charities/documents/research-and-development-tax-credit-guidelines.pdf). A tax adviser should confirm the law and claim treatment for the company's accounting period.

## Executive summary

The repository shows several credible candidates for technical R&D in the period, particularly:

1. Query-result caching and correctness/invalidation.
2. Multinode failure detection, recovery and session safety.
3. Read/write routing across primary and replica databases.
4. Server admission control and adaptive client throttling.
5. PostgreSQL `java.sql.Array` support across the driver, gRPC transport and backend JDBC driver.

Prepared-statement-cache translation and streaming/performance work are additional candidates, but need stronger evidence of uncertainty and systematic experiments. New language clients and the September architecture-only proposals are lower-confidence candidates: adopting an existing protocol or documenting a future design is not automatically R&D.

This is a screening of repository activity, not a determination that OJP or any contributor qualifies. GitHub history can show when code and documents changed, but not who performed the work, how much time they spent, what knowledge was available to them, or which company bore the cost. No eligible expenditure can be calculated from this evidence.

## Work not recommended for the R&D activity inventory

The following may be necessary and valuable business work, but repository evidence alone does not make them qualifying R&D:

- Routine bug fixes, dependency/CVE updates, refactoring, release automation, versioning, CI setup, packaging and ordinary compatibility fixes.
- Documentation restructuring, tutorials, user guides and routine test expansion. Project records and test results can support an R&D claim, but producing documents or tests is not automatically qualifying activity.
- Ordinary configuration, integration of known vendor settings, and implementation of a well-established solution without technological uncertainty.
- Work completed before 1 March 2026. For example, the query-caching analysis is dated 11 February 2026; only evidenced work performed within the specified period should be considered.
- Any work after 6 October 2026 is outside this review's evidence cut-off, although it may fall within the supplied claim period.

## Opinions and suggestions

- **Prioritize the evidence for caching, admission/throttling, read/write routing, multinode recovery and PostgreSQL arrays.** These have the clearest in-period implementation evidence and plausible technical uncertainties.
- **Do not treat commits, lines of code or feature novelty as proof of R&D.** A competent-professional analysis should explain why the solution was not readily deducible from publicly available knowledge at the time.
- **Build the claim from project records, then allocate time and cost.** Preserve dated hypotheses, alternatives considered, experiments, test/benchmark results, failures, design changes and technical conclusions. Map those records to named people and time spent on qualifying activities versus routine work.
- **Separate eligible expenditure from activity qualification.** Payroll, contractor, subcontracting, consumables and other cost treatment depends on the claimant, contracts, payment records and applicable rules; get Irish tax advice before including any amounts.
- **Keep a dated update for October 2026–February 2027.** Do not estimate future qualifying work now. Reassess actual activities and preserve evidence contemporaneously.

## Questions to resolve before a claim

1. Is 1 March 2026–28 February 2027 the company's accounting period, and which company would make the claim?
2. Which contributors were employees, contractors or volunteers, and are dated project-level time records and supporting payroll/invoice records available?
3. Are there contemporaneous benchmark results, failed experiments, technical decision records, issues or review discussions that explain the uncertainties behind the repository changes?
4. Were relevant activities performed outside the public repository or by related parties, and who bore their costs?
